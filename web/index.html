What Life OS is for

Organising your own life — the work you owe other people, the things you keep meaning to get to, and the few habits you are trying to hold on to.

It is built around one idea: the question worth answering every morning is what does today actually look like, and no single app usually knows. Your meetings are in one place, your tasks in another, and the thing you promised yourself you would do every day is in a third.

So Life OS puts your real calendar next to your real work. It knows Thursday already has three meetings before you promise yourself an afternoon of deep work. It keeps your diary and your notes beside the work they are about, and asks nothing of you beyond writing things down.

It is free. There is no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking. Nothing you write is read, sold, or used to train anything. Life OS is built and run by one person — Zander van der Berg, in South Africa.

What you can do with it

Seven surfaces, one workspace. Nothing here is a separate app you have to keep in step.

Today
The work actually in front of you, sorted into today, this week and this month — instead of one endless list of everything you have ever written down. It is the screen Life OS opens on.
Calendar
Your real Google Calendar, with the time you have set aside for your own tasks shown beside your meetings. Create, move and delete events without leaving Life OS.
Projects
Bigger things broken into steps, so a project is never just a word on a list you feel guilty about.
Diary
Somewhere to write the day down — private, searchable, and beside the work it refers to.
Library
Notes, ideas and reference kept as books with sections, so you can find something again six months later.
Habits
The few things you want to do regularly, ticked off without ceremony, with the streak kept quietly in the background.
Reminders
The things that are not tasks and do not belong in a calendar — renew the passport, ring the dentist.

Connecting Google Calendar

Optional, reversible, and explained in full before you agree to anything.

What Life OS asks Google for

  • See the events in your calendars So your meetings and your tasks appear on one screen, and Life OS can tell you when something you are planning collides with something you have already agreed to.
  • Create, change and delete events So an event you make in Life OS is a real event in Google Calendar. Life OS only ever writes the specific change you confirm on screen first — it never edits your calendar in the background.
  • See the list of calendars you have So you can choose which calendar a new event belongs to, rather than everything landing in one.
  • See when you are free or busy So it can warn you before you schedule work over something else.

What it never does

  • Write to your calendar without you confirming that exact change
  • Create, delete or re-share a calendar
  • Read your email, contacts, files or location
  • Sell, share or advertise against anything it holds
  • Require a calendar at all — Life OS works fully without one

Life OS keeps a copy of your events so the calendar loads instantly and can spot clashes without asking Google every time. Disconnecting removes that copy. The full detail is in the Privacy Policy.
