What Life OS is for

Life OS is for organising your own life — the work you owe other people, the things you keep meaning to get to, and the handful of habits you are trying to hold on to. It is built around one idea: that the question worth answering every morning is what does today actually look like, and that no single app usually knows.

So Life OS puts your real calendar next to your real tasks. It knows that Thursday already has three meetings before you try to promise yourself an afternoon of deep work. It keeps your diary and your notes in the same place as the work they are about, and it asks nothing of you beyond writing things down.

It is free, there is no advertising, and nothing you write is read, sold, or used to train anything. Life OS is built and run by one person — Zander van der Berg, in South Africa.

What you can do with it

Today
The work actually in front of you, sorted into today, this week and this month — instead of one endless list of everything you have ever written down.
Calendar
Your real Google Calendar, with the time you have set aside for your own tasks shown beside your meetings. Create, move and delete events without leaving Life OS.
Projects
Bigger things broken into steps, so a project is never just a word on a list you feel guilty about.
Diary
Somewhere to write the day down, kept private, searchable, and beside the work it refers to.
Library
Notes, ideas and reference kept as books with sections, so you can find something again six months later.
Habits
The few things you want to do regularly, ticked off without ceremony, with the streak kept quietly in the background.
Reminders
The things that are not tasks and do not belong in a calendar — renew the passport, ring the dentist.

Connecting Google Calendar

Life OS can connect to your Google Calendar so your real schedule appears alongside your work, and so you can create and change events without switching apps. This is entirely optional — you can create an account and use everything else without it, and you can disconnect at any time from Settings.

If you connect it, Life OS asks Google for permission to:

  • See the events in your calendars So your meetings and your tasks appear on one screen, and Life OS can tell you when something you are planning collides with something you have already agreed to.
  • Create, change and delete events So an event you make in Life OS is a real event in Google Calendar. Life OS only ever writes the specific change you confirm on screen first — it never edits your calendar in the background.
  • See the list of calendars you have So you can choose which calendar a new event belongs to, rather than everything landing in one.
  • See when you are free or busy So it can warn you before you schedule work over something else.

Life OS keeps a copy of your events so the calendar loads instantly and can spot clashes without asking Google every time. Disconnecting removes that copy. Exactly what is stored, and for how long, is set out in the Privacy Policy.
